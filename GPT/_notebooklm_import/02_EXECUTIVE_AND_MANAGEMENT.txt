# Executive Positioning

## Core frame

Anton should be positioned as a technology and transformation leader who combines systems thinking, implementation ownership, stakeholder communication, adoption support, and hands-on delivery range.

He is not best framed as a developer who later picked up management tasks.
He is stronger as a leader who can also go deep technically when the situation requires it.

## Primary identity options

- CTO
- Head of Engineering
- Head of IT
- Engineering Manager
- Solutions / Systems Architect with delivery ownership
- Digital Transformation or Implementation Lead

## Core value proposition

Anton enters messy technical or operational environments, maps the real workflow, identifies structural risks, formalizes hidden or informal processes, and turns fragmented execution into a system that can be run, governed, and maintained.

## Main themes to emphasize

1. Technical and operational leadership
2. Implementation ownership
3. Process and system design
4. Cross-functional coordination
5. Governance, security, and support logic
6. Rollout, adoption, and training
7. Architecture as a business and delivery competency
8. Calm execution in ambiguous environments
9. AI-assisted execution management where relevant

## Best summary language

Prefer language such as:

- technology leader
- implementation owner
- systems architect
- delivery leader
- cross-functional operator
- operational transformation
- stakeholder-heavy environment
- source-of-truth systems
- adoption and implementation
- governance and adoption
- modernization without operational breakage

## Stack rule

Use stack and tooling only as evidence of depth and credibility.
Do not let tooling become the identity.

## Recommended one-line positioning

Anton Nazarov is a technology and implementation leader who helps organizations turn fragmented operations, weak tooling, and delivery chaos into structured systems that teams can run, govern, and scale.

# Management Experience Cases

This file exists to surface leadership and implementation ownership proof that can be missed when the profile is read only through a developer lens.

## Case 1. ZIL Cultural Center

### Best-fit labels

- Head of IT
- CIO-like operational leader
- infrastructure and support leader
- modernization and governance owner

### What actually happened

Anton ran the IT function for a large cultural organization with around 200 PCs, 7 servers, about 20 virtual machines, CCTV, telephony, access systems, and related building infrastructure.

He managed 5 IT staff and 2 installation engineers, controlled operating and modernization budgets, selected vendors and contractors, and communicated with higher-level public authorities.

### Leadership proof

- reorganized the department and its documentation
- launched a HelpDesk for IT and seven additional support services
- rebuilt service discipline and request transparency through structured HelpDesk governance
- operated in a politically sensitive anti-crisis environment with internal resistance and legacy power structures
- moved 100+ employees to remote work during the pandemic
- introduced Active Directory and security policies
- audited information security and personal-data handling
- prepared remediation proposals and implemented them
- coordinated asset, licensing, and documentation cleanup
- developed departmental regulations, role descriptions, and internal procedures
- supervised third-party development of a new website
- designed business-process algorithms for future automation
- used HelpDesk transparency not only for support, but also to protect the IT team and make workload visible to management
- built an operating model that remained stable after his departure

### What this proves

- full-function IT leadership
- operational governance
- service-management design
- people leadership
- vendor coordination
- security and continuity ownership
- modernization under real organizational constraints
