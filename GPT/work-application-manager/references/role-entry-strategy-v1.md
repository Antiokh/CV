# Role-entry strategy based on interview evidence

This is the current candidate-side role targeting and entry-path strategy for Anton Nazarov. It is based on Anton's explicit synthesis of recent interview outcomes as of 2026-09-04. It governs vacancy prioritization and positioning, not factual Fit % inflation.

## Core distinction

Do not collapse these into one score:

1. **Role relevance / Fit** — whether Anton has credible evidence for the work itself.
2. **Cold-entry probability** — how likely the first recruiter/technical filters are to recognize that fit from his current chronology and recent hands-on history.
3. **Strategic value after entry** — whether the role provides a credible path into broader product/engineering/technology leadership inside the company.

A role can be highly relevant but a poor cold-entry path. Conversely, a Product or applied-AI role can be strategically valuable partly because it gets Anton inside the company through evidence that is easier to validate externally.

Never lower or inflate Fit % solely because the entry channel is easier or harder. Use entry probability for search/application priority and outreach strategy.

### Primary cold-application track: Applied AI Engineering / AI Principal

Recent interview experience indicates strong relevance where AI engineering is paired with analytical, architectural, automation, product or delivery judgment.

Positioning logic:

- emphasize current AI-assisted engineering, agent/repository guardrails, AI automation, system integration, data/workflow reasoning, product/technical analysis and delivery;
- strongest target roles are applied AI / AI tooling / AI automation / AI-enabled product engineering / principal-level AI delivery roles where broad systems judgment matters;
- do not imply deep ML research/model-training specialization when the evidence is actually applied AI, automation, architecture, tooling and product delivery;

Strategic implication: an applied-AI engineering entry can also lead toward wider technical/product leadership because it validates current technical relevance through the part of engineering where Anton is actively working now.

### High role relevance, selective cold-entry: Engineering Manager

Anton has relevant management/engineering-leadership evidence, but recent interview outcomes show a recurring first-filter risk when the role expects very recent conventional hands-on software-development depth or stack-specific coding readiness.

Prioritize EM vacancies when one or more are true:

- the role emphasizes team/system ownership, delivery, architecture, operating model, hiring, prioritization, technical judgment, cross-functional work or AI adoption more than daily coding;
- the company values broad product/technical management rather than a recently promoted senior developer profile;
- there is a warm path, referral, recruiter relationship, internal trust or other channel that allows the full management evidence to be evaluated before a narrow stack-recency filter;
- AI/tooling/automation is materially part of the engineering scope.

Deprioritize cold EM roles whose decisive filter is recent deep implementation in a conventional stack unrelated to Anton's current hands-on work.

### High role relevance, trust-weighted entry: CTO / Head of Engineering / technology leadership

Anton has strong CTO/technology-leadership evidence in business outcomes, architecture, operational control, teams, vendors, infrastructure, adoption and transformation.

The main issue is not relevance but external trust and interpretation of the career path. For these roles, prefer:

- warm introductions, founder/recruiter relationships and network paths;
- companies where business/technology transformation, product/AI strategy, architecture, operating control or organizational design is the real pain;
- roles where the CTO is not screened primarily as the best current individual contributor in one contemporary programming stack;
- internal progression after Anton has established credibility through Product or applied-AI work when that path is available.

Do not stop applying cold when the vacancy clearly matches the evidence. The rule is selective prioritization, not exclusion.

### Lower cold priority: generic non-AI technical lead / IC-heavy engineering leadership

Recent interview evidence suggests weak conversion for roles where the primary proof expected is very recent conventional software-development depth in a specific stack.

Do not spend disproportionate search/application effort on generic Tech Lead / hands-on engineering-lead roles without AI unless the vacancy clearly values Anton's architecture, systems, product or management evidence enough to offset stack-recency risk.

This is an entry-probability rule, not a statement that Anton lacks technical competence.

## Non-linear career positioning

Do not frame Anton's moves between banking systems, product ownership, IT leadership, CTO/system architecture and applied AI as a lack of focus.

The recurring pattern is domain shift followed by concrete achievement in each domain. Applications should show the relevant sequence of proof for the target role, not defend the entire career.

Useful framing principle:

**Anton has repeatedly moved into adjacent technology domains, learned the operating model, and produced measurable outcomes. The current application should prove the target-domain sequence rather than explain every transition.**

For Product roles, show the product-development lineage. For AI roles, show current applied-AI delivery plus the underlying architecture/automation/product judgment. For management roles, show systems changed through decisions, people, ownership and operating structure.

## Search and prioritization rules

When otherwise comparable vacancies exist, prioritize application effort approximately in this order:

1. Product Manager / technical product roles with substantive ownership, discovery, analytics and cross-functional delivery;
2. applied AI Engineering / AI Principal roles where analytics, automation, architecture, tooling or product judgment matter;
3. Engineering Manager roles with low dependence on recent stack-specific coding and high dependence on leadership/system judgment, especially with a warm path or AI scope;
4. CTO / Head / technology-leadership roles with strong pain/evidence match, prioritizing trust-based channels while still allowing strong cold matches;
5. generic non-AI Tech Lead / IC-heavy leadership roles only when unusually well matched.

This order affects time allocation and application priority, not Fit %.

## Company-entry strategy

When a strong company has multiple credible paths, do not automatically choose the grandest title.

Prefer the role that:

- has strong evidence-backed fit;
- passes the likely first-screen filters cleanly;
- gives Anton access to the company and meaningful product/technical ownership;
- leaves room for responsibility to expand after trust is established.

Product and applied-AI roles can therefore be strategically superior entry points to an externally harder CTO/EM application at the same company.

Do not misrepresent the target role as temporary or tell the employer Anton intends to leave it immediately. Apply sincerely to the job being offered while recognizing internal mobility as a strategic benefit.

## Application-positioning interaction

Always apply `application-positioning-v1.md` first for employer pain -> proof.

Then use this role-entry strategy to choose which evidence sequence is easiest for the employer to trust:

- Product: problem discovery -> product decision -> adoption/business result;
- Applied AI: analytical decomposition -> AI/automation/system implementation -> verified operational result;
- EM: team/system problem -> leadership/architecture/process decision -> delivery/operating result;
- CTO: business/technology problem -> ownership/organization/architecture decision -> business, risk, continuity or control result.

Do not make applications about being versatile. Versatility is useful only when the selected proof demonstrates the employer's problem can be solved.
