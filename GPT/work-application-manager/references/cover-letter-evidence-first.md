# Evidence-first cover letter contract

This is the current cover-letter operating contract for Anton Nazarov's candidate-side applications.

## Purpose

A cover letter is not a motivation essay and not a biography. Its job is to make the recruiter conclude: **this candidate satisfies the requirements, and here is the evidence**.

The letter must behave like a compact requirement-to-proof mapping written as natural human prose.

## Mandatory drafting order

Before drafting, extract the vacancy into three buckets:

1. **Nice-to-have / preferred experience**
2. **Essential / required experience**
3. **Filters and risk points** such as years, company size, domain, language, geography, work authorization, title history, specific tools, or management scope

Then draft in this order:

1. Lead with the strongest verified nice-to-have evidence. If several nice-to-haves are supported, surface them before generic essentials.
2. Walk through the essential requirements. Every material requirement should be answered with evidence: what Anton did, where, at what scale, and with what result when a verified result exists.
3. Explicitly close filter risks from the correct angle. If the title differs but the work is directly applicable, say so and prove the underlying work. Do not hide a likely filter mismatch behind generic wording.
4. End only when the evidence case is complete. A closing sentence should be short and role-specific; it must not repeat generic enthusiasm.

## Evidence standard

Each paragraph or bullet should contain both:

- a recognizable vacancy requirement or preferred criterion; and
- at least one concrete proof item from verified experience.

Strong proof includes:

- named role, employer, product or project;
- scale: users, institutions, team size, records, systems, clients;
- measurable before/after result;
- exact responsibility: discovery, prioritization, specification, architecture, rollout, analytics, adoption, hiring, stakeholder work;
- technologies used when the vacancy asks for technical fluency.

Delete any sentence that only says Anton is interested, capable, comfortable, passionate, experienced, organized, strategic, technical, collaborative, data-driven, or a strong fit without proving it.

## Nice-to-have priority

Nice-to-have evidence is disproportionately valuable because it differentiates candidates who already pass the basics. When Anton genuinely has a preferred criterion, surface it early instead of burying it after generic qualifications.

Examples: exact domain adjacency, booking/scheduling, Figma, a requested platform, a language, a small-company environment, specific analytics tooling, regulated industry experience, or a matching customer type.

Never invent a nice-to-have. If a preferred item is unsupported, omit it or identify it as a gap during internal fit analysis; do not turn the cover into a disclaimer list.

## Filter handling

For explicit filters such as `3+ years`, do not merely repeat a number. Establish the time span and the directly applicable work.

Example pattern:

> The formal titles varied, but the product work did not: since 2017 I have owned discovery, requirements, prioritization, delivery and adoption for live operational products, including ...

Use this pattern only when the underlying evidence supports it.

## Forbidden generic openings and filler

Do not open or pad cover letters with stock corporate phrases. Unless Anton explicitly requests a conventional style, avoid phrases such as:

- `My background ...`
- `I'm interested in this role because ...`
- `I am excited to apply ...`
- `I believe I am a strong fit ...`
- `My experience aligns well with ...`
- `I bring X years of experience ...` without immediate requirement-specific proof
- `I am passionate about ...`
- `This opportunity ...`
- `I would love the chance ...`
- `I am comfortable with ...` when concrete use can be stated instead
- `I have experience with ...` when a stronger `I built / led / owned / shipped / analyzed / redesigned` statement is available

Also avoid praise of the company or vacancy text unless it conveys unique, evidence-backed motivation that materially strengthens the application.

## Voice

Use direct, factual, confident language. Prefer active verbs: `owned`, `built`, `led`, `mapped`, `redesigned`, `shipped`, `measured`, `migrated`, `reduced`, `increased`, `trained`, `interviewed`, `specified`.

The letter may use bullets when that makes requirement coverage clearer. Do not force traditional letter prose if a compact evidence matrix reads better for the company and application channel.

Apply the language-specific humanizer after the evidence structure is complete. Humanization must remove artificial phrasing without deleting requirement coverage or weakening concrete proof.

## Final QA

Before finalizing, ask:

- Did the opening use the strongest relevant nice-to-have proof available?
- Can a recruiter map every essential requirement to at least one proof point?
- Are explicit filters such as years, small-company experience, technical fluency, domain or geography visibly closed?
- Does every paragraph add new information?
- Are there any generic self-descriptions that could be replaced by a fact?
- Are any metrics, titles, tools, domains or responsibilities invented or overstated?

If a sentence could be sent unchanged by hundreds of other applicants, remove or rewrite it.
