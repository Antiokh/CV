# Evidence-first cover letter contract

This is the current cover-letter operating contract for Anton Nazarov's candidate-side applications.

It is subordinate to and must be used together with `application-positioning-v1.md`. If this file conflicts with that contract, the pain-first positioning contract wins.

## Purpose

A cover letter is not a motivation essay, biography, requirement checklist, or researched company summary.

Its job is to make the hiring manager conclude:

**this candidate understands the problem we are hiring someone to solve, has solved the same or a structurally similar problem before, and can prove it.**

Requirements remain an important QA surface, but they are not automatically the prose structure.

## Mandatory drafting order

Before drafting:

1. Build the Pain Map required by `application-positioning-v1.md`: 1-3 hiring pains, desired changed state, filters/risks, strongest verified proofs.
2. Search canonical evidence before writing. For product and managerial roles, start from the business-result evidence in `RESUME_FRACTIONAL_CTO.md`, then deepen from management/product/project sources when needed.
3. Select normally two or three strong proof cases. Prefer a case that closes several requirements at once over one generic sentence per requirement.
4. Check Nice-to-have / preferred experience and hard requirements as risk filters. Surface an exact nice-to-have early only when it is unusually strong evidence for the core hiring pain.
5. Draft around the employer's problem and the proof, then run the requirement-coverage audit after the narrative is coherent.

## Default cover structure

### 1. Open on the problem

The first substantive sentence should normally identify the problem pattern visible in the vacancy and connect it immediately to a relevant case.

Good opening logic:

- `You are trying to make a service-business workflow scalable without losing customer signal. I dealt with that directly in Dobri Visarun...`
- `This role reads less like "manage an IT team" and more like "restore ownership and make operations measurable." At ZIL...`

These are structural examples only. Use the actual vacancy language and verified case evidence.

Do not open with Anton's biography, years, enthusiasm, generic fit, or company praise.

### 2. Give 2-3 proof cases

Each proof should contain as much of this chain as the evidence supports:

**problem / situation -> Anton's decision or action -> result / changed state -> relevance to this role.**

Strong proof includes:

- named role, employer, product or project;
- scale: users, institutions, team size, records, systems, clients;
- measurable before/after result;
- exact responsibility: discovery, prioritization, specification, architecture, rollout, analytics, adoption, hiring, stakeholder work;
- technologies only when they matter to the hiring risk.

Do not repeat the same case three different ways to make the letter look complete.

### 3. Close filters compactly

After the main proof, audit the vacancy's hard filters:

- years of directly applicable work;
- management scope;
- technical depth;
- company size / startup / enterprise exposure;
- domain;
- language / geography / work authorization;
- exact tools or platforms when truly mandatory.

If the proof cases already close a filter, do not restate it generically. Add a short sentence only for material filters that remain ambiguous.

### 4. End without filler

A short role-specific next step is enough. Do not repeat excitement or summarize the whole letter.

## Requirements are QA, not a checklist template

After drafting, compare the letter against every material requirement and preferred criterion.

Every central requirement should be:

- visibly supported by a proof case;
- covered by adjacent truthful evidence;
- or recognized internally as a real gap.

Do not add one bullet per requirement merely to achieve visual completeness. A single strong case can demonstrate discovery, prioritization, technical fluency, cross-functional work and business impact at once.

## Nice-to-have handling

Nice-to-have evidence is valuable because it can reduce uncertainty quickly, especially exact domain/customer/workflow matches.

But "nice-to-have first" is **not** a mechanical rule.

Lead with it when it is the strongest proof of the employer's core problem. Do not lead with Figma, a tool, or a minor domain adjacency if a stronger business/problem case should dominate the opening.

Never invent a nice-to-have. Unsupported preferred items remain gaps.

## Evidence standard

Delete any sentence that only says Anton is interested, capable, comfortable, passionate, experienced, organized, strategic, technical, collaborative, data-driven, or a strong fit without proving it.

Prefer supported evidence from the master CV and canonical sources. Use numbers, before/after states and observable operational changes aggressively where verified.

Do not turn external web research into cover-letter content unless it materially clarifies the hiring problem. Market statistics, company praise, funding facts, generic growth claims and citations normally do not belong in the letter.

## Forbidden generic openings and filler

Unless Anton explicitly requests a conventional style, avoid phrases such as:

- `My background ...`
- `I'm interested in this role because ...`
- `I am excited to apply ...`
- `I believe I am a strong fit ...`
- `My experience aligns well with ...`
- `I bring X years of experience ...` without immediate role-specific proof
- `I am passionate about ...`
- `This opportunity ...`
- `I would love the chance ...`
- `I am comfortable with ...` when concrete use can be stated instead
- `I have experience with ...` when `I built / led / owned / diagnosed / redesigned / shipped / measured` is available

Also avoid praise of the company or vacancy unless it conveys unique, evidence-backed motivation that materially improves the argument.

## Voice

Use direct, factual language. Prefer active verbs tied to events and outcomes: `owned`, `diagnosed`, `mapped`, `redesigned`, `built`, `led`, `prioritized`, `shipped`, `measured`, `migrated`, `reduced`, `increased`, `restored`, `stabilized`, `trained`, `restructured`.

Bullets are allowed when they improve clarity, but a cover should not read like a compliance matrix unless the application channel specifically rewards that format.

Apply the language-specific humanizer only after the Pain Map and proof structure are complete. Humanization must not remove pain recognition, requirement coverage, numbers, or concrete proof.

## Final QA

Before finalizing, ask:

- What problem does the employer appear to be hiring this person to solve?
- Which vacancy wording supports that interpretation?
- Does the opening address that problem instead of introducing Anton?
- Are the two or three strongest verified proof cases visible?
- Does each case contain a real action/decision and changed state/result?
- Are hard filters covered without turning the letter into a list of requirements?
- Did I surface a nice-to-have because it is high-value proof, not because of a rigid rule?
- Did I preserve strong business-result evidence from `RESUME_FRACTIONAL_CTO.md` when relevant?
- Did I include internet/company trivia that does not strengthen solution fit? Remove it.
- Are there generic self-descriptions that could be replaced by facts?
- Is any metric, title, tool, domain, responsibility, or causal claim invented or overstated?
- Could a paragraph be sent unchanged by hundreds of applicants after changing the company name? If yes, remove or rewrite it.
