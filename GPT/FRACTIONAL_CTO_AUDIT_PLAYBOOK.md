# Fractional CTO & Technology Operations Audit Playbook

## Purpose

This file captures Anton Nazarov's reusable executive technology-management framework for CTO, CIO, Head of IT, Digital Transformation and Fractional CTO positioning.

It must be presented as a **problem-solving operating model**, not as a claim that Anton is simply experienced or technically strong.

The positioning shift is:

**from "I am good at technology" to "I recognise what is hurting the business, I know what to inspect first, and I know what to do next."**

Do not mention confidential prospects or companies used to develop this framework.

## 1. Executive problem pattern

The framework fits organisations where technology already matters to business operations but management has lost visibility, predictability or control.

Common symptoms:

- software delivery stalls while development cost rises;
- releases are delayed and nobody can explain the true bottleneck;
- IT spend is difficult to connect to business capability or measurable outcome;
- the real process is fragmented across CRM, spreadsheets, email, chat and individual memory;
- a critical handoff fails because someone forgot, got sick, did not know or did not have time;
- employees, teams or vendors pass responsibility between one another;
- workload, queues and hidden underperformance are difficult to see objectively;
- important systems depend on one employee, one contractor or one vendor;
- CRM exists but daily work continues outside it;
- management cannot see where website or customer journeys lose conversion;
- integrations are undocumented, manually reconciled or operationally fragile;
- security, continuity and vendor risk are reviewed separately from business decisions;
- management knows technology needs attention but does not yet know what should be kept, improved, integrated, replaced or added.

The executive value is not a tool list. It is the ability to make these problems observable, owned and economically actionable.

## 2. Business-outcome hierarchy

### Transparency

Make visible:

- workload and queues;
- process lead time and bottlenecks;
- system usage;
- customer drop-off;
- ownership;
- technology spend;
- recurring incidents;
- manual work;
- critical dependencies;
- security and continuity risk.

Purpose: find where money, time and decision quality leak.

### Efficiency

Use that visibility to:

- automate routine work with AI and deterministic workflows;
- improve workforce utilisation;
- remove duplicate systems, licences and vendors;
- reduce re-entry and reconciliation;
- reduce incidents and support effort;
- simplify process bottlenecks;
- integrate systems end-to-end;
- reduce key-person and vendor dependency;
- reuse internal capability before buying more.

### Growth

After the operating model becomes measurable:

- improve CRM and sales productivity;
- recover digital conversion losses;
- improve customer/partner journeys;
- use AI to increase throughput or service quality;
- reuse technology across departments or companies;
- evaluate whether differentiated internal technology or IP can become a shared service or commercial asset.

Sequence:

**Transparency -> Efficiency -> Growth**

## 3. Core management principles

### Single-point ownership

Every critical process, system, data domain, integration, vendor relationship and material risk should have one named accountable owner.

This is not blame management. It prevents responsibility ping-pong and makes cost, decisions and outcomes traceable.

### Operational visibility

Management should see where work waits, what repeats, who owns the next action, how much effort is consumed and which business result is affected.

### IT budget transparency

Material technology spend should be connected to:

`capability -> owner -> cost -> users -> business value -> risk -> lifecycle`

This turns technical jargon into a financial management view and shows where money is being spent without enough value.

### Seamless system integration

Critical business processes should not stop because somebody forgot, got sick, did not know or did not have time.

Automate system-to-system handoffs so state, data and responsibility continue without avoidable manual intervention.

### AI-driven routine automation

Use AI for concrete repetitive work such as:

- triage and classification;
- knowledge search;
- draft responses;
- document processing;
- extraction and reconciliation;
- reporting preparation;
- repetitive bounded decisions.

Do not present AI as a strategy by itself. Tie it to a process, baseline and measurable effect.

### Low-dependency architecture

Prefer technology choices that reduce critical switching cost and structural dependency:

- open standards;
- portable data;
- explicit APIs;
- controlled source code and accounts;
- reproducible deployment;
- documented recovery;
- replaceable components where practical;
- vendor exit paths.

Do not claim zero vendor lock-in. The goal is to minimise critical dependency and preserve options.

### Technology as a business asset

Technology is not only a cost centre.

Ask:

- can internal capability replace external spend;
- can it be reused across departments/business units;
- does differentiated IP exist;
- can it become a shared internal platform or service;
- is any part mature enough to justify external commercialisation.

Treat this as an evaluation, not an assumption.

### Stabilise before transforming

Do not begin by assuming everything must be changed.

Understand what works, what is fragile, what is expensive, what is risky and what management actually needs. Preserve continuity while improving the system.

### Metrics before dashboards

First define the decision, metric, source, calculation, owner and action. Only then decide whether a new analytics platform is required.

### Prove practices inside IT, then scale outward

Use the technology function as the first controlled environment for ownership, service management, risk, metrics and operating discipline. Scale successful practices into business processes only after they work.

## 4. First 30 days — Understand & Stabilise

Priority: leadership, team, current fires and baseline visibility.

Actions:

- spend maximum attention on CEO/owner, COO, CFO and relevant business leaders;
- identify their priorities, doubts, current technology pain and existing commitments;
- identify which problems consume executive attention today;
- assess the IT team: real roles, skills, workload, SOPs, backup coverage and key-person dependencies;
- understand current incidents, fires and commitments that cannot be paused;
- review architecture, documentation, contracts, vendors and technology spend at a practical level;
- map critical applications, services, data flows and dependencies;
- establish an initial information-security, continuity and third-party risk baseline;
- assess CRM rollout, IT support flow, digital analytics coverage and shadow workflows;
- configure/validate event tracking for key digital journeys where visibility is missing;
- capture baseline metrics for IT workload, incidents, website conversion, CRM usage, critical risks and technology spend;
- create a prioritised value backlog: quick savings, growth experiments, urgent risks and items requiring a proper business case.

Avoid in the first 30 days:

- a large reorganisation;
- enterprise-wide Process Ownership;
- a predetermined platform replacement;
- a new BI platform before metrics and ownership are defined.

Expected result:

**Leadership knows what is burning, what IT actually owns, who can solve it, where the largest risks sit and which decisions should come next.**

## 5. First 100 days — Put IT under control

Priority: make the technology function manageable and measurable.

Actions may include:

- audit the information-system landscape;
- create an application and critical-service inventory;
- inventory APIs, ESB, files, direct database links, scheduled jobs and manual transfers;
- implement Service Desk and lightweight ITIL practices;
- formalise Incident / Request / Problem / Change handling;
- define a service catalogue and basic service ownership;
- introduce monitoring and observability;
- make assets, identities and access visible;
- improve documentation and operational knowledge;
- validate backup, restore, continuity and recoverability;
- create a risk register with owners, treatment and acceptance;
- make vendor ownership and exit/continuity controls explicit;
- stabilise CRM adoption, usability, core integrations and reporting;
- establish a clear IT <-> business communication rhythm;
- assess team capability and correct roles where required;
- work with HR where formal role, responsibility or personnel changes are required;
- build a lightweight staff-assessment tool only if current HR systems are insufficient;
- establish a Business & IT scorecard with concrete management metrics.

Expected result:

**IT stops being a black box and becomes a function with visible work, ownership, cost, service level and risk.**

## 6. Six months — Move into business processes

After IT itself becomes manageable, apply proven practices to priority business domains.

Actions may include:

- trace the highest-value business journeys end-to-end;
- analyse process lead time, waiting, handoffs, rework and bottlenecks;
- establish Process Ownership and System Ownership;
- connect process outcomes to systems, data, cost and KPIs;
- automate the highest-friction spreadsheet, mailbox and copy-paste workflows;
- eliminate avoidable manual system handoffs;
- establish integration monitoring and reconciliation controls;
- optimise CRM, Contact Center, Sales, Finance, Operations or E-commerce where evidence shows value;
- introduce domain data ownership and common KPI definitions;
- quantify run-cost savings, released capacity and revenue opportunities by domain;
- collect successful operating practices and scale them outside IT.

Expected result:

**The company can see and improve the processes where money, capacity and customer experience are lost.**

## 7. One year — Scale to company / Group level

At this stage the role moves from controlling the technology function to shaping the wider operating model.

Actions may include:

- company/Group Process, System, Data, Integration and Risk Ownership;
- common architecture, API, data and security principles;
- application portfolio rationalisation;
- standardisation of shared capabilities where the economic case is proven;
- justified local/country adapters where needed;
- retirement of duplicate spend;
- scaled automation, analytics and AI on governed data and measurable processes;
- CRM and digital optimisation against conversion, retention and productivity metrics;
- reuse of internal technology/IP across business units;
- evaluation of external commercial potential where internal technology is genuinely differentiated;
- a continuous transformation portfolio with business-owned financial outcomes.

Expected result:

**Technology is transparent enough to see the money, efficient enough to reduce waste and flexible enough to support growth.**

## 8. Audit / management domains

### Leadership priorities

Questions:

- Which outcomes matter most to leadership?
- Which technology problems already reach executive level?
- Which commitments are already in flight?
- Where does leadership lack confidence in ownership or numbers?

### Team and capability

Assess:

- actual role versus title;
- workload and queues;
- capability gaps;
- key-person dependency;
- duplicated responsibility;
- senior people doing routine work;
- internal versus vendor capability;
- leadership work that should be delegated;
- alignment between responsibilities and operating model.

### IT service management

Target flow:

`request / incident -> service desk -> owner + priority -> resolution -> knowledge -> problem / change`

Keep familiar channels where useful, but meaningful work becomes a managed record.

### Information Security & Risk Management

Assess at minimum:

- critical services/assets;
- sensitive/regulated data;
- privileged access;
- third parties and key-person dependency;
- IAM/MFA/privileged controls;
- vulnerability/patching;
- secure configuration;
- backup coverage and restore testing;
- RTO/RPO;
- environment rebuildability;
- incident response;
- business continuity/DR;
- vendor exit controls.

Prioritise by business impact, probability, recoverability and exposure.

### CRM and employee usability

Treat CRM as an operating process, not a software deployment.

Assess:

- share of work actually performed in CRM;
- time per contact;
- clicks and re-entry;
- search/navigation;
- training burden;
- escalation/follow-up;
- reporting and handoffs;
- ERP/order/e-commerce integration;
- duplicates/data quality;
- role model and adoption ownership.

### Digital journey and feedback

Instrument meaningful journeys such as registration, product discovery, search, cart, checkout, payment and post-purchase.

Connect behavioural analytics with customer questions, support signals and AI-assistant topics so findings feed a prioritised UX/process backlog.

### Integration and process continuity

For every critical interface capture:

`source -> target -> owner -> data -> frequency -> monitoring -> failure handling -> reconciliation`

The business purpose is less manual work, fewer missed handoffs, shorter lead time, lower reconciliation cost and less key-person dependency.

### Application portfolio and spend

For every material system capture:

`Owner | Cost | Users | Business capability | Value | Risk | Integrations | Lifecycle`

Then classify:

- **KEEP** — proven strategic asset;
- **IMPROVE** — useful core with fixable friction;
- **INTEGRATE** — valuable capability isolated by workflow/data boundaries;
- **REPLACE** — proven blocker with unacceptable cost/risk/lifecycle/scalability;
- **ADD** — missing capability needed to control, measure or grow.

Decision criteria:

- business value;
- risk;
- run/change cost;
- lifecycle;
- integration burden;
- data quality;
- scalability;
- recoverability;
- switching cost/vendor dependency.

### Data and management visibility

Create common KPI definitions and ownership before committing to a new analytics platform.

A management view should answer:

- Where do we lose revenue or margin?
- Where do we spend technology money?
- Where is work waiting?
- Which systems create incidents or manual work?
- Which risks can stop the business?
- Which initiative actually improved the result?

## 9. Cost and risk reduction mechanisms

When explaining Anton's value, use mechanisms rather than unsupported outcome promises.

1. **AI-driven routine automation** — reduce repetitive manual work and free capacity for higher-value judgement.
2. **Workforce visibility and utilisation** — expose queues, overloaded roles, underused capacity and hidden dependency so growth does not automatically mean more headcount.
3. **Process bottleneck optimisation** — measure waiting, handoffs, rework, lead time and dependencies.
4. **Technology-spend transparency** — connect systems, licences, vendors, infrastructure and internal effort to business capability, owner, value and risk.
5. **Resilient, portable, low-dependency technology** — reduce switching cost and key vendor/specialist dependency through explicit boundaries, portable data, controlled source/accounts, reproducible operations and recovery plans.
6. **End-to-end system reintegration and automation** — connect fragmented information systems so processes continue without relying on someone to remember, know, be available or manually transfer the next state.

## 10. Management metrics

Do not use "dashboard" as an empty promise. Name the decisions and metrics.

### Executive view

- revenue / margin leakage;
- technology spend by capability;
- transformation ROI;
- critical business risks;
- market/business-unit comparison.

### Operational view

- workload and backlog;
- process lead time and bottlenecks;
- incidents and recurring problems;
- integration failures / reconciliation;
- CRM adoption and manual re-entry;
- digital funnel performance.

### Decision & action

For every material exception:

- exception/problem;
- named owner;
- corrective action;
- priority/due date;
- expected saving, revenue effect or risk reduction.

### Transparency / management control

Examples:

- % critical processes with named owner;
- % critical systems with named owner;
- % technology spend mapped to system/capability;
- % critical integrations with monitoring and failure owner;
- % critical services with tested recovery;
- % key digital journey covered by event analytics.

### Service

Examples:

- incoming incidents/requests;
- backlog;
- resolution time;
- SLA where applicable;
- recurring incidents;
- workload by service/team.

### Customer / commercial

Examples:

- conversion / checkout success;
- activation;
- sales productivity;
- repeat purchase / retention;
- repeat contacts;
- resolution time;
- journey success;
- CSAT / feedback.

## 11. Typical audit deliverables

Depending on scope:

1. Executive problem and priority map.
2. Current-state business/technology map.
3. IT team and capability assessment.
4. Critical application/service inventory.
5. Integration and dependency map.
6. Technology spend/vendor visibility model.
7. Security, continuity and third-party risk register.
8. CRM/digital analytics/support-flow findings.
9. Business & IT baseline scorecard.
10. Ownership register.
11. Prioritised value backlog.
12. Keep / Improve / Integrate / Replace / Add portfolio classification where evidence supports it.
13. First 30 / 100 day action plan.
14. Six-month / one-year roadmap where scope supports it.
15. Business cases for selected savings, risk or growth initiatives when enough baseline data exists.

## 12. Investment logic

### PROTECT

Security, continuity, recoverability and critical lifecycle risk.

### SIMPLIFY

ITSM, integration, technical debt, workflow, automation and portfolio rationalisation.

### GROW

CRM, digital experience, analytics, AI, business automation and technology products where a business case exists.

Every material investment should have:

- owner;
- baseline;
- expected revenue uplift, cost saving or risk reduction;
- measurable review point.

## 13. Executive positioning rule

For CTO/CIO/Head of IT/Fractional CTO materials, do not lead with:

- "18 years of experience";
- technology stacks;
- generic claims of leadership;
- "I define / I design" without economic or operating consequence.

Lead with a recognisable business problem and a mechanism.

Pattern:

`pain -> consequence -> what Anton makes visible/owned -> what changes next -> proof`

Examples:

### Instead of

> Technology leader with extensive experience in architecture and transformation.

### Prefer

> When delivery slows, costs rise and nobody can show where work is actually stuck, Anton makes the technology operating model visible: ownership, workload, systems, spend and risk. He then stabilises the critical function before deciding what to automate, integrate, replace or scale.

### Instead of

> Strong experience in automation and integrations.

### Prefer

> Anton removes operational handoffs that depend on someone remembering, being available or moving data manually between systems, replacing them with owned and observable system-to-system workflows.

### Instead of

> Experienced in IT management.

### Prefer

> Anton turns IT from a black box into a measurable function: requests become managed work, systems and risks get owners, spend maps to capabilities, and management can see where time and money are being lost.

## 15. Guardrails

- Do not mention the confidential company or hiring case from which this framework was refined.
- Do not assume everything must be replaced.
- Do not attack an existing team, CTO or vendor.
- Do not promise a quantified saving or revenue uplift before a baseline exists.
- Do not promise zero vendor lock-in.
- Do not imply employee surveillance or automatic headcount reduction.
- Do not sell AI without a concrete workflow and measurable outcome.
- Do not move into company-wide process redesign before the technology function itself is understood and manageable.
- Preserve operating continuity during transformation.
