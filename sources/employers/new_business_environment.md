## Why this role matters

This is a short but strategically useful commercial implementation / adoption case. Anton joined a company implementing ERP/CRM systems on PlanFix to understand why technically delivered automation was often failing to become a working management tool for clients.

The strongest signal is not `PlanFix experience`; it is **diagnosing implementation failure after technical delivery** and reframing success around adoption, ownership and operating change.

## Business problem

The company delivered ERP/CRM automation projects, but larger clients could buy and technically receive systems without actually embedding them into daily work.

Observed consequences included weak:

- user adoption;
- management engagement;
- post-launch ownership;
- support / expansion potential;
- retention / LTV;
- repeatable delivery quality.

Anton was brought in to understand where implementation was failing and how delivery / adoption could be made more sustainable.

## Client-side implementation analysis

Anton analyzed situations where:

- users did not understand the system;
- managers were insufficiently involved;
- employees resisted or ignored process change;
- interfaces / workflows required too much explanation;
- responsibilities for adoption were unclear;
- technical delivery was treated as the end of the project.

He participated in:

- implementation-methodology work;
- training;
- manager coaching;
- quality review of proposed solutions;
- process changes;
- testing adoption / involvement hypotheses.

## Core diagnosis

Anton connected failed adoption to several recurring operating issues:

- unclear ownership between delivery team and client;
- weak client communication;
- missing post-launch responsibility;
- a gap between technical acceptance and actual operating use;
- organizational role ambiguity.

This supports the framing:

> Shifted implementation thinking from `system delivered` toward adoption, managerial ownership and measurable business effect.

## Internal operating-model observations

Anton observed that a nominally flat / highly consensus-driven structure could create a responsibility vacuum when final accountability was unclear.

His recommendation was not to remove collaboration, but to make responsibility explicit:

- people can discuss / contribute;
- each domain needs an accountable owner;
- final decisions need a clear decision-maker.

This is useful evidence for organizational design / delivery governance, but should be phrased neutrally in public materials.

## Technical vs project leadership dependency

Anton also observed a delivery risk where technical knowledge / access was overly concentrated and project leadership lacked enough visibility into technical execution.

Consequences included difficulty with:

- schedule control;
- quality control;
- client accountability;
- scaling implementations;
- reducing dependency on one technical person.

This is useful as evidence of recognizing key-person / transparency risk inside delivery organizations.

## Founder advisory / manager coaching

Anton worked with founders and managers on:

- implementation methodology;
- responsibility boundaries;
- delivery model;
- client communication;
- post-launch ownership;
- adoption strategy.

Do not overstate this as formal executive authority over the company; it was an implementation / product role with advisory value.
