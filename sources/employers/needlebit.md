## Technology-investment / platform judgment

A recurring pattern is evaluating technology by **TCO, product ceiling, supportability, portability and future change**, not tool preference.

### Glide → WeWeb

Anton advised / moved client work away from Glide when customization limits constrained the product. WeWeb enabled richer capabilities including:

- map-heavy interfaces;
- themes;
- more controllable authentication flows;
- a proper desktop-oriented experience.

Signal: platform choice based on future product requirements / ceiling.

### Supabase instead of Airtable

In one client project Anton recommended and designed a **Supabase** backend instead of Airtable for a structured long-lived backend.

Outcome:

- frontend was later completely rewritten;
- Supabase backend remained in production;
- Anton recalls approximately **three years** of continued backend operation.

Signal: separation of presentation and core data/service layer, avoiding unnecessary replatforming.

### Production Xano system and later backend replacement

In a separate case, the system did reach production on **Xano** even though Anton had recommended another direction.

Later, using Anton's architecture / documentation, the client replaced **the entire Xano backend** with an agentically developed alternative.

Signal:

- handoff quality;
- portability;
- reduced platform / key-person dependency;
- architecture understandable enough to reimplement after Anton.

Do not conflate this with another story where a client insisted on Xano despite Anton warning about cost / speed.

### Bubble / Odoo / other platform caution

Anton has discouraged platforms where he expected predictable rewrite, support or ownership problems. Use only project-specific examples when the exact context is supported. General claim: evaluates build/buy/platform choices through long-term ownership cost and exit path.
