## Why this role matters

ZIL is Anton's strongest classic CIO / Head of IT / transformation case because it combines:

- direct line management;
- operating-model redesign;
- anti-crisis cost pressure;
- IT economics and procurement;
- service-management transformation;
- business continuity;
- security and identity governance;
- cross-department workflow automation;
- vendor / platform decisions;
- public-sector procurement constraints;
- executive stakeholder representation;
- team development and delegation;
- creation of a technology function that remained operational after Anton left.

The role should not be reduced to workstation upgrades, HelpDesk administration or password recovery. Those were implementation mechanisms inside broader management decisions.

## Strategic mandate / management context

Anton joined the management team during an anti-crisis period. The technology mandate had several layers:

1. reduce operating cost and support wider internal optimization;
2. stabilize a poorly controlled / distrusted IT function;
3. close security, continuity and access risks;
4. make service work observable and dependable;
5. use the recovered trust/capacity to digitize processes outside IT;
6. help reposition ZIL as a more modern, technology-forward and youth-oriented cultural venue;
7. enable new digital event formats during COVID, including paid streaming.

Anton deliberately started by fixing IT itself: broader digital transformation had little credibility while core technology operations were unreliable.

## Direct team and organizational design

Largest verified direct span in Anton's career:

- **5 IT staff + 2 installation engineers**;
- vendors and contractors managed in addition to internal staff.

Anton redesigned capability around differentiated ownership rather than generic `sysadmin/support` roles. The model included combinations of:

- Service Desk / documentation / business-systems ownership;
- on-site workplace / endpoint support;
- web / digital / design capability;
- deeper infrastructure / DevOps expertise;
- installation / physical-infrastructure capability.

A senior infrastructure / DevOps capability was used fractionally / remotely where deeper expertise was required but a full-time equivalent was economically unnecessary.

Anton usually diagnosed the problem, defined the approach with the relevant specialist, launched the process and then delegated steady-state execution while retaining accountability for economics, risk and priorities.

## People management

- Rewrote responsibilities and clarified ownership.
- Introduced measurable service expectations / KPI-oriented management.
- Made personnel changes where inherited performance did not meet operating needs.
- Hired, onboarded, trained and developed staff.
- Developed people from nontraditional backgrounds when capability matched the role; one employee moved from sales/public-administration background into IT documentation / CRM / process work, another developed capability around cybersecurity / hardware.
- Designed adjacent backup coverage for critical functions instead of making everyone interchangeable generalists.
- Managed resistance to clearer accountability and service standards.
- The operating model continued functioning for approximately **18 months after Anton's departure**, which is strong evidence that the system did not depend only on him personally.

Do not claim long-term team retention beyond what evidence supports.

## Economics and anti-crisis contribution

ZIL had approximately **RUB 30m in accumulated debt** during the wider anti-crisis period. Anton was part of the management team contributing to internal optimization; do not attribute the entire debt turnaround solely to IT.

Strong technology-economics evidence:

- chose lifecycle extension over blanket workstation replacement;
- targeted upgrades / SSD replacement and licensing strategy for approximately **150 workstations**;
- estimated avoided workstation-replacement and licensing cost approximately **RUB 22m**;
- evaluated capability cost, including fractional expert staffing rather than automatically hiring every competency full-time;
- evaluated procurement by total transaction cost, not unit price alone.

The key signal is technology-capital allocation and TCO judgment, not `installed SSDs`.

## Procurement and vendor management

Anton managed the IT procurement lifecycle under **223-FZ** constraints:

- supplier / solution search;
- requirements / specification drafting;

He challenged embedded / overpriced positions where rational, but also avoided splitting procurement when the extra tender/procurement effort and delay would cost the organization more than the price difference being optimized.

Anton negotiated discounts and free trial periods for software / specialized CRM where possible.

Historical remembered total IT procurement value of roughly RUB7–8m is not sufficiently corroborated for hard CV use.

## Platform / CRM judgment

### Bitrix24 → Pyrus

- ZIL spent / committed approximately **RUB 90k** on Bitrix24 during CRM experimentation.
- User testing showed it was too complex for the actual adoption context.
- Anton stopped further investment instead of defending the sunk cost.
- Process / workflow work shifted toward **Pyrus**, which had a lower adoption threshold for users.

Do not frame RUB90k as savings; it was already spent. The signal is stopping further investment in a poor-fit platform.

### Odoo evaluation

Anton evaluated Odoo as a nominally free / self-hosted option but rejected it because total ownership cost was unattractive in the actual environment:

- resource-heavy relative to available infrastructure;
- customization / integrations required Python capability;
- internal capability for that stack was limited;
- changes would be slower / more expensive than alternatives.

This supports build/buy/TCO judgment.

## Service-management transformation

Before redesign, IT support was heavily informal: calls, personal messages, corridor conversations and tasks that could remain unresolved for months / years. A technician could spend half a day walking between users, and there was little trustworthy service visibility.

Anton first used a broad intake / complaint collection to surface backlog and categorize/prioritize demand, then moved work into structured HelpDesk / ITSM.

After redesign:

- HelpDesk became the system of record;
- request types and ownership were documented;
- prioritization considered urgency + business impact;
- both **reaction-time and resolution-time** expectations were defined;
- routine issues were often resolved remotely in approximately **10–15 minutes**;
- relevant SLA classes had targets around **30 minutes**;
- monthly demand was roughly **300–600 requests** initially and fell to about **150/month** over approximately 6–12 months as backlog was cleared, endpoints standardized and recurring user problems reduced through training / root-cause work.

The decline in ticket volume should not be framed as `users stopped reporting`. Evidence supports backlog reduction, standardization, user training and reduced recurring demand.

## Service analytics / root-cause work

Ticket analysis showed many `IT problems` were actually:

- usability questions;
- professional-software usage questions;
- training gaps;
- process issues rather than failures.

Anton used the service queue to distinguish real failures from repeated demand that could be removed through education, standardization or process change.

## Extending service-management beyond IT

The controlled workflow pattern was reused outside IT. Historical evidence supports extension to **seven additional internal services**.

This is useful as a cross-functional service-management signal; do not make it the headline for CTO roles.

## Event-operations workflow

Before digitization, recurring functions such as IT, lighting, sound and installation / technical setup maintained separate Excel copies and coordinated via phone/email. There was no single request entity, shared history or reliable resource conflict visibility.

Anton / team created a shared event-request workflow with:

- one request record;
- shared calendar / planning grid;
- equipment reservation;
- ownership;
- status/history;
- statistics / traceability.

Approximately **300 events** passed through the process during practical use.

Adoption was not perfect; management turnover / retraining and some fallback to phone remained. Do not claim total process elimination.

## Cross-department process automation

Technology / workflow changes extended into or touched:

- finance / accounting: financing requests;
- legal: contract initiation / procurement requests;
- procurement: document preparation / coordination;
- technical operations: service workflows;
- HR / asset / finance interactions around accounts, inventory and responsibilities.

Anton often rewrote adjoining operating procedures where IT workflows depended on HR, accounting, legal or procurement behavior.

## Business continuity / endpoint operating model

Anton moved the organization away from dependence on individual PCs / local files:

- centralized identities through Active Directory;
- centralized / backed-up network storage;
- standardized endpoints;
- reduced dependence on personal laptops and local data;
- a failed workstation could be swapped and the user continue with the same identity / centrally stored data.

During COVID Anton led the move of **100+ employees** to secure remote work while maintaining operations.

## Key-person continuity failure and privileged access

Inherited infrastructure was excessively dependent on one administrator with broad access and insufficient shared documentation / credential ownership. After that person left / conflict occurred, the organization lost access to some credentials, operational knowledge and systems/data.

Anton recovered critical control and rebuilt:

- privileged-access ownership;
- credential/documentation practices;
- shared system knowledge;
- responsibility / continuity rules.

Public CV-safe framing:

> Recovered critical IT control after a key-person continuity failure and rebuilt privileged-access, documentation and ownership practices so core systems no longer depended on one administrator.

Do not include allegations about sabotage, theft or personal motives in public materials.

## Identity lifecycle / offboarding

Anton found more than **160 mailboxes / accounts associated with former employees** still active; in some cases former employees could retain access after leaving.

He rebuilt joiner / mover / leaver procedures across IT and adjacent HR processes:

- HR-to-IT synchronization;
- stale-account monitoring;
- account disablement / removal during offboarding;
- clearer ownership of access lifecycle.

This is one of the strongest CIO / security-governance signals.

## Security and observability

Anton audited and addressed problems around:

- personal-data handling;
- RBAC / access rights;
- endpoints;
- infrastructure monitoring;
- user / public-web visibility;
- information-leakage risk / DLP-style controls;
- public-computer privacy/session reset;
- shared storage and permissions;
- security procedures / documentation.

The intended operating principle was effectively `no dark corners`: resources, users, sites and critical controls should be observable and owned.

## Licenses, certificates and renewal governance

A concrete failure involved an expired certificate disrupting financial / accounting reporting.

Anton moved licenses, keys and certificates into GLPI-style lifecycle tracking with reminders far enough in advance (roughly three months where appropriate) to start procurement / renewal before expiry.

The signal is transition from emergency renewal after failure to planned lifecycle management.

## Change resistance and leadership behavior

Several changes initially faced resistance:

- named accounts;
- centralized storage;
- security policies;
- HelpDesk use;
- backups;
- standard workstation rules.

Some users described stronger controls as a `digital prison`. Over time the model normalized and expectations of IT reliability increased substantially.

Anton also:

- pushed back on ad-hoc exceptions requested by influential stakeholders;
- defended role clarity (e.g. not treating graphic/product designers automatically as web developers);
- reframed debt-crisis staff fear toward productivity / capability density rather than indiscriminate cuts.

Use these as interview/change-management evidence rather than inflammatory CV copy.
