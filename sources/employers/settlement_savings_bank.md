## Why this role matters

This is Anton's strongest early **process-governance → system → management-control** case. The work should not be reduced to `built a Lotus Notes document system`.

The bank had document-routing and deadline problems with regulatory / financial consequences. Anton worked across executives and departments to formalize how documents were supposed to move, resolve disagreements before automation, and make ownership / delay visible in the resulting system.

## Business / regulatory context

Before the new workflow system:

- documents could be delayed or lost inside internal routing;
- reporting / required submissions could miss deadlines;
- management could not easily see where a document had stalled;
- departments could dispute whether / when they had received a document;
- physical movement of originals / paper created additional opacity and delay.

Anton recalls financial losses / penalties connected to missed deadlines. The preserved evidence supports **regulatory-risk reduction**, but not a specific proven amount of fines avoided.

Historical external legal research showed that late / improper submissions to the Bank of Russia could carry substantial penalties in 2014. This is legal context only and must never be converted into a claim that Anton saved that exact amount.

## Governance before automation

When department heads disagreed about the correct route, Anton did not encode the ambiguity into software.

The pattern was:

1. bring conflicting stakeholders together;
2. escalate unresolved operating rules to the chairman / board governance where needed;
3. document the agreed process in detail;
4. only then implement it.

This is strong executive / product / delivery evidence: **resolve operating ownership before automating the workflow**.

## System built

Anton designed and built an internal electronic document-management system on IBM Lotus Notes / Domino with XML-based **1C integration**.

The system tracked each transition and made visible:

- when a document entered the system;
- who received it;
- how long it stayed with a department / owner;
- when it moved to the next stage;
- whether it was overdue;
- where a bottleneck was occurring.

The system covered approximately **60 back-office users / most back-office staff**.

## Operating outcome

After rollout, Anton recalls:

- internal documents **stopped disappearing** in the workflow;
- ownership and overdue status became traceable;
- multiple people could work from digital copies / the same matter without physically circulating the original;
- management could see actual processing time and identify bottlenecks;
- routine internal courier circulation was no longer necessary; courier work remained for **external physical delivery**.

These are stronger CV outcomes than generic `workflow automation`.

## Bottleneck discovery

The visibility exposed that some recurring delays occurred at the initial intake / secretariat / registration stage rather than only in specialist departments.

Raw recollection includes a later personnel decision involving the acting head of the secretariat. Do not use that personnel detail in CV copy. The safe signal is that system telemetry / workflow visibility exposed an unexpected bottleneck and supported management action.
