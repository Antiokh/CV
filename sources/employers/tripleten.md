# TripleTen — CRM Automation Manager (short engagement evidence dossier)

**Organisation:** TripleTen — Product Automations, US and LATAM markets  
**Formal title:** CRM Automation Manager  
**Period:** Late October–early November 2026 (documented activity through 3 November; exact HR start/end dates unverified)  
**Engagement type:** Short operational assessment and initial implementation, approximately one to two weeks  
**Evidence status:** Repository-backed technical artifacts, dated research and first-person account  
**Career use:** Selective supporting evidence, **not** a default entry in the chronological senior-management CV.

## Business context and mandate

The immediate work focused on understanding the Product Automations function as an operating system: where business requests arrived, how they were qualified, how weekly sprint commitments were recorded, which teams owned decisions and handoffs, and what information management could reliably use to evaluate throughput, responsiveness and cost.

In parallel, I examined the software portfolio and licensing model to locate potential duplication, unnecessary entitlements, alternatives and vendor-consolidation opportunities. This was an early diagnostic, not a completed company-wide transformation or savings programme.

## Evidence-backed work completed

### Delivery operating model and workload visibility

- Reconstructed request intake and delivery paths from available Notion planning data, Jira histories, Slack channels and technical artifacts, noting unclear ownership and cross-team handoffs.
- Identified a planning/reporting discrepancy: `Weekly Sprint` can represent a delivery commitment even when the `Deadline` field is empty. A deadline-only capacity report therefore misses some planned work.
- Developed Clockify–Notion project/task classification and synchronization tooling to distinguish planned development from support and interruptions. No validated organisation-wide hours baseline was established during the engagement.

### Continuity and knowledge governance

- Organised a version-controlled Git/Obsidian knowledge base with linked business-process drafts, automation records, system inventories, source provenance and outstanding validation questions.
- Assessed the operational risk of workflow knowledge being fragmented across tools and individual contributors. The evidence did not establish that all company documentation was missing or that a critical production outage had occurred.
- Improved Make blueprint archiving and incremental-change tooling, providing better inspection and handover of automation configurations; complete disaster-recovery readiness was **not** established.

### Software economics and access control

- Reviewed system ownership, available SaaS licensing information and selected seat-allocation anomalies, distinguishing assigned access from verified active use.
- Designed a normalized systems–licenses–accounts–seat-history model and an initial capability-based comparison method: retain, downgrade, reassign, consolidate, replace or unbundle software where total cost justified it.
- The full cross-vendor capability matrix, contract reconciliation and quantifiable savings assessment were **not completed**. No realized license savings should be attributed to this engagement.

### Cross-team systems and implementation groundwork

- Investigated service-desk routing and stakeholder ownership, including whether existing support tooling could responsibly serve as a universal employee request channel.
- Built initial Supabase operational schemas and integration components; prepared a process-aware request portal and Make-to-n8n migration research/prototypes. These were at different stages of implementation and were not a completed production rollout.
- Preserved research, source code, configuration metadata and explicit recovery gaps in a dated technical handover.

## Result and limits

**Delivered:** An evidence-backed diagnostic of delivery operations and software economics, traceable knowledge and technical assets, and a concrete starting point for ownership clarification, measurement, continuity planning and vendor decisions.

**Not measured or delivered:** Reduced company spend, improved SLAs, faster team delivery, company-wide adoption of a new operating model, fully verified restoration, or a production Make-to-n8n migration.

The value of this short engagement is **rapid operational diagnosis connected to hands-on execution**. It illustrates a Fractional CTO-style skill set; it does **not** mean the formal role was CTO, CIO, a board appointment, or an executive mandate over company technology budgets.
