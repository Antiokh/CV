## 1. Strategic mandate at ZIL

Anton described three primary objectives for his role, plus one prerequisite he imposed before broader transformation work.

### Objective 1 — financial recovery through internal optimization

- Help the organization address its debt burden by finding internal optimization opportunities.
- Reduce payroll / operating load where functions could be transferred from manual work to IT products and automation.
- Treat technology as an instrument for reducing recurring operating cost, not only as infrastructure support.

### Objective 2 — make ZIL technologically progressive

- Make ZIL a more advanced and progressive cultural venue by integrating current technology ideas and enabling them technically.
- During COVID, ZIL began offering **paid event broadcasts / streaming** as a digital format when physical attendance was constrained.
- Anton actively participated in idea competitions and initiatives from the Moscow Department of Culture and describes himself as one of the more productive contributors.
- He proposed AI/personalization concepts even when the available technology and economics were not yet mature enough for implementation.
- Example concept: displays with cameras that would adapt cultural-event recommendations to the approaching visitor. Anton notes that a personalized cultural profile was later implemented elsewhere in the ecosystem, but **no causal claim should be made that this later product was derived from his proposal unless documentary evidence is found**.

### Objective 3 — make ZIL feel like a modern, youth-oriented venue

- Use digital products and technology as part of ZIL's public positioning and visitor experience.
- The new corporate website was part of this objective.
- Anton's role in the website initiative included idea generation, evaluation of concepts and technical execution / delivery involvement.

### Prerequisite — rebuild the IT function before transforming other departments

Anton explicitly states that he argued broader transformation would fail if IT itself remained poorly controlled or distrusted.

His sequence was:

1. identify and close the internal IT gaps;
2. make IT reliable, observable and governed;
3. rebuild credibility with the rest of the organization;
4. use that credibility and operating capacity to transform other departments.

This is important executive evidence because it frames the IT reconstruction as **a deliberate prerequisite for organizational transformation**, not as an end in itself.

## 4. Cost optimization and technology economics

### Workstation lifecycle and licensing

Anton clarifies that the workstation-upgrade story was a budget strategy, not a hands-on hardware achievement.

His account:

- the organization faced widespread equipment wear and a likely need for mass replacement;
- instead of purchasing replacement computers at roughly RUB 70k–100k each, the team used targeted low-cost upgrades where economically justified;
- this avoided approximately **RUB 16m** in replacement spending;
- Windows migration / licensing strategy avoided approximately another **RUB 6m**;
- combined potential avoided cost: approximately **RUB 22m**.

Until underlying calculations or procurement documents are stored, these figures should be treated as **user-confirmed estimates**.

The executive interpretation is:

- lifecycle management;
- CAPEX avoidance;
- licensing strategy;
- choosing the economically rational modernization path instead of blanket replacement.

### Procurement under 223-FZ

Anton states that he directly handled / closed procurement procedures under **223-FZ**, including:

- checking procurement specifications for embedded / "locked-in" positions and unjustified price inflation;
- evaluating not only the purchase price of an item but also the **transaction and organizational cost of running the tender process itself**;
- recognizing cases where accepting an expensive item inside an existing procurement could be economically preferable to splitting it into a separate procurement with additional administrative cost, delay and organizational load.

This is evidence of **total-cost decision-making**, not just procurement administration.

Anton states that he was a **regular representative of ZIL Cultural Center in higher-level organizations, including the Moscow Department of Information Technologies (DIT)**.

In that capacity he:

- represented the Cultural Center in technology-related interaction with parent / city organizations;
- directly implemented technologies and platforms they provided;
- deliberately used available centralized capabilities where building an equivalent solution internally would create unnecessary cost or organizational load;
- reduced the burden on the small internal IT function by integrating external / city-provided technology rather than duplicating it in-house.

This is evidence of:

- stakeholder representation at higher organizational levels;
- build-vs-reuse judgment;
- platform / ecosystem integration;
- cost-conscious technology strategy;
- leverage of shared public-sector capabilities under tight budget constraints.

## 10. Executive pattern supported by this debrief

The ZIL role should not be summarized as a collection of infrastructure tasks. The evidence supports the following sequence:

1. **Business mandate:** reduce cost, modernize the venue and strengthen its digital position.
2. **Diagnosis:** audit technology, security, licensing, service operations, compliance, systems and staff capability.
3. **Stabilization:** close critical security / personal-data / access / infrastructure gaps.
4. **Operating model:** build a lean team, clear ownership, governance, documentation and observability.
5. **Economics:** avoid unnecessary CAPEX and licensing spend; optimize procurement by total transaction cost.
6. **Trust:** rebuild the credibility of IT inside the organization.
7. **Transformation:** use that credibility to digitize workflows across departments and launch new digital formats.
8. **External leverage:** represent ZIL upward and integrate centrally provided technologies rather than rebuilding everything internally.
9. **Innovation:** contribute product / AI / personalization ideas and new digital audience experiences.

A useful short framing is:

> Rebuilt an under-controlled IT function into a trusted transformation capability, using technology to reduce cost, control risk, modernize operations and enable new digital products under severe budget constraints.
