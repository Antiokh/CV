## Strength Categories

### Realised Strengths

#### Communicator
Traits: Charismatic, clarifying, considerate, tactful.

Meaningful use:
Ensures shared understanding by summarizing clearly, choosing precise words, and aligning interpretations.

Overuse risk:
May over-explain and overwhelm others. A useful counterbalance is to pause and ask what the other person actually needs to know.

#### Examiner
Traits: Analytical, attentive, objective, prudent.

Meaningful use:
Safeguards quality by verifying information, catching inconsistencies, and spotting risks early.

Overuse risk:
May overcheck and drift into perfectionism. A useful counterbalance is to define the required accuracy level in advance.

#### Inventor
Traits: Courageous, creative, independent, proactive.

Meaningful use:
Creates novelty by generating original solutions, connecting ideas, and running experiments.

Overuse risk:
May open too many ideas at once and hurt delivery. A useful counterbalance is to limit experiments and tie them to current priorities.

### Unrealised Strengths

#### Motivator

#### Networker
Builds connections by initiating contact, staying visible, and creating useful relationships.

#### Facilitator
Enables participation and alignment by helping groups include voices, work through tension, and adapt together.

## Working Interpretation

The profile points to a person who communicates clearly, thinks critically, generates new approaches, learns fast, and is comfortable closing decisions. In work terms, this fits roles that require system-level thinking, structured communication, quality control, and technical or organizational leadership.

The profile also suggests a practical tradeoff: strong communication, analysis, and invention are valuable, but they need deliberate limits to avoid over-explaining, perfectionism, or too many parallel ideas.
