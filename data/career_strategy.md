# Career Strategy

This document captures a practical strategy for earning more while avoiding the burnout of low-value freelance work.

It is intended as a canonical reference for:
- long-term positioning
- compensation strategy
- role prioritization
- client-facing proof
- deciding which work to accept and which to avoid

## Core Objective

The objective is not "more gigs".
The objective is:

- higher-value roles
- higher ownership
- higher leverage
- less context switching
- less client chasing
- more predictable income

The strongest path is to be paid for:
- judgment
- structure
- ownership
- leadership
- system design
- implementation and adoption
- turning chaos into execution

---

## Best Money Zones

### 1. Fractional CTO / Interim CTO

Best when:
- the company is messy
- a founder needs a senior technical brain
- architecture and delivery need cleanup
- the company will pay for judgment, not just coding

Why it pays:
- high ambiguity
- seniority premium
- ownership premium

### 2. Engineering Manager

Best when:
- there is real team responsibility
- delivery is stuck
- merger / reorg / chaos exists
- the company needs someone who can coordinate, not just code

Why it pays:
- leadership + delivery + execution
- strong fit with ZIL / Directorate / bank / New Business Environment evidence

### 3. Systems Architect / Solutions Architect

Best when:
- system complexity is high
- internal platforms or workflow systems are broken
- someone needs to design the fix, not just implement tickets

Why it pays:
- architecture is expensive when it is real
- bank + Directorate + AIS evidence is highly relevant

### 4. Technical Product Lead / Product Owner

Best when:
- product and engineering overlap
- workflows, reporting, and operational logic matter
- the company needs structure, not just backlog management

Why it pays:
- strong in global companies
- fits MetaFox / Dobri / PromptlessPress evidence

---

## Work To Avoid

Avoid:
- low-value gig work
- short churny projects with no ownership
- pure ticket-factory dev work
- generic low-code freelancing
- roles where you are only an executor and never a decision-maker

These use your energy badly.

---

## Strength Usage Strategy

### Realised strengths to lean on
- Communicator
- Examiner
- Inventor
- Decision Maker
- management / systems thinking

Use them for:
- leadership
- architecture
- product framing
- negotiation
- diagnosis

### Unrealised strengths to use more often
- Motivator
- Networker
- Facilitator

Use them for:
- interviews
- stakeholder alignment
- team buy-in
- client trust
- moving a company through ambiguity

These are not weaknesses; they are leverage.

---

## Energy Rule

Do not maximize output at the cost of permanent fatigue.

Avoid roles that constantly drag you into draining modes:
- repetitive service work
- endless small-client management
- under-owned roles
- shallow freelance tasks
- constant multitasking with no strategic authority

---

## Time Horizon Strategy

### Short term
Use selective applications and high-signal positioning:
- EM / Product Leader / Fractional CTO / Architect
- ask for strong money
- use bank / ZIL / Directorate / New Business Environment evidence
- do not underprice

### Medium term
Move toward:
- a stable lead role
- a higher-ownership role
- a product/engineering leadership seat
- or a fractional CTO style engagement with fewer, better clients

### Long term
Build a market identity as:
- a systems / engineering / product leader
- who can enter chaos and create order

Not:
- just a freelancer
- just a developer
- just a manager

---

## Best Roles for Money + Fit + Sustainability

1. Fractional CTO / Interim CTO
2. Engineering Manager
3. Systems Architect / Solutions Architect
4. Technical Product Lead
5. Product Owner / Product Lead

This order balances:
- pay potential
- fit
- ability to prove value fast
- lower burnout than traditional freelance work

---

## External Positioning

Use a market-facing line like:

> I help companies turn messy technical and operational situations into structured systems, with a mix of engineering leadership, architecture, implementation ownership, adoption support, and AI-assisted delivery.

This supports:
- employment
- advisory roles
- fractional roles
- selective consulting
- product leadership

without locking you into a freelance identity.

---

## Practical Client-Facing Summary

If a client wants to know why you are useful, the simplest proof story is:

- he can build from scratch
- he can lead teams
- he can restructure broken systems
- he can translate business chaos into technical execution
- he can work in both product and engineering contexts

That makes him more than a developer: he is a delivery and systems owner.
