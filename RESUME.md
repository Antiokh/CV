## Technology Investment & Architecture Judgment

A recurring part of my work is telling people that the technically possible choice is not necessarily the sensible one. I look at supportability, adoption, internal capability, future change and exit cost, not only the feature list or license price.

- At ZIL, stopped further investment in **Bitrix24 after approximately RUB 90k had already been spent** when testing showed the platform was too complex for the actual user base; shifted process work toward Pyrus instead of defending a sunk-cost choice.
- Evaluated Odoo as a self-hosted/free option but rejected it when infrastructure demand, customization requirements and the need for specialized Python development made the real ownership cost unattractive.
- Advised clients away from Glide when customization ceilings constrained the product; moving to WeWeb enabled map-heavy interfaces, themes, better authentication flows and a proper desktop experience.
- Recommended Supabase instead of Airtable for a structured backend. That backend has remained in production for approximately **three years** and survived a complete frontend rewrite without replacement of the core data layer.
- Delivered a production system on Xano even though I had recommended another direction. The architecture and documentation were explicit enough that the client later replaced **the entire Xano backend** with an agentically developed alternative.
- Prefer pilots and progressive rollout before major commitment. I treat adoption and operational fit as investment criteria rather than assuming that a product with more features is automatically the better choice.
- Have rewritten failed externally developed systems when continuing to patch them would have cost more than replacing the underlying design.
